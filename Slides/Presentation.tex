\section{EBA \& SSM}
\frame{\frametitle{The EBA's EU-wide Transparency Exercise  \cite{EBA6} \cite{guideline}}

\begin{itemize}


\item The European Banking Authority (EBA) is an independent EU Authority established on 1 January 2011 as part of the European System of Financial Supervision (ESFS) to ensure effective and consistent prudential regulation and supervision across the European banking sector.

% \item  With the EU-wide transparency exercise, the EBA has been disclosing detailed bank-by-bank data on  an annual basis since 2016, with the objective of providing the general public and market participants with updated information on the financial conditions of EU banks at the highest level of consolidation.

\item  With the EU-wide transparency exercise, the EBA has been disclosing data on  an annual basis since 2016, which:
   
   \begin{itemize}
   
    \item  Provides the general public and market participants with updated (quaterly/semesterly) information on the financial conditions of EU banks at the highest level of consolidation

   \item Discloses an extensive, and granular portfolio-by-portfolio supervisory  data on major European banks -exposures are broken down by banks, asset classes, default status, ect..-. representing approximately between 80\% and 85\% of banking assets in the European Union.
   
   \item Uses an harmonised definition of NPLs across European countries.
   
   \item Includes data on original exposures up to the 10 largest countries of counterparty at bank level
    \item Provides information regarding membership status under the Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM)
   
    \end{itemize}

%\cite{Kerbl}\cite{Turk}
 
 
 \end{itemize}

\frame{\frametitle{Cross-Border Exposure-Weighted Macroeconomic Indicators \cite{Quagliariello} \cite{Fuchs}}

\begin{itemize} 

\item To determine the macroeconomic drivers on NPLs  for  banks with  a cross-border portfolio using data provided on a domestically controlled consolidation basis, the use of cross-border exposure-weighted macroeconomic indicators was required
%cross-border 
\item The following formula was applied to calculate the Cross-Border Exposure-Weights:

\begin{align*}
y^\ast_{it} = \sum_{l=0}^{N_{it}} w^t_{il}Y_{lt} \qquad \text{with}\qquad \sum_{l=0}^{N_{it}} w^t_{il}= 1
\end{align*}
\\
where $w^t_{il}$ is the cross-border exposure-weight of bank $i$ from country $l$ at time  $t$, $Y_{lt}$ is the macroeconomic variable of country $l$ at time $t$ and $y^\ast_{it}$ is the cross-border exposure-weighted macroeconomic indicator for bank $i$ at time $t$. The  value of $N_{it}$  varies depending on the number of countries with the largest cross-border exposures for each bank $i$ at time $t$ with $\max(N)= 10$.


\end{itemize} 
}


\frame{\frametitle{Cross-Border Diversification Index $(CBDI)$ \cite{Morgan.2016} \cite{Hayden.2006}  \cite{BergerA.N.&DeYoungR.2001}}

\begin{itemize} 

\item A geographic diversification index of bank exposures is also introduced into the model in order to account for the effects of cross-border diversification  on non-performing loans.
%\cite{TuDQ.SonHTran.2020}.

\item   The Cross-Border Diversification Index ($CBDI$) has been calculated as  the inverse of the the Hirschmann-Herfindahl Index (HHI) for each bank $i$ at time $t$. Formally,

\[
 CBDI_{it} = 1 - HHI_{it}
\]
with
\[
  HHI_{it} =\sum_{j=0}^{N_{it}}( \dfrac{X_{ijt}}{X_{it}})
\]

where $X_{j}$ measures  the original exposure towards country $j$  and $N_{it}$ represents the number of countries with the largest cross-border exposures for each bank $i$ at time $t$ with $\max(N)= 10$. The Hirschmann-Herfindahl indexes are bounded by $1/N_{it} \leq HHI_{it} \leq 1$.

\item  The Cross-Border Diversification Indexes ($CBDI$) are bounded by $0 \leq CBDI_{it} \leq (N_{it} -1)/N_{it}$. A Cross-Border Diversification Index ($CBDI$) close to its maximum means that the bank original exposures are very diversified across countries   whereas an index close to zero means that the bank original exposures are very concentrated on certain countries

\end{itemize} 
}

\frame{\frametitle{Linear  \& GMM Panel Models \cite{Baltagi.2005} \cite{Arellano.Bover.1995}}

\begin{itemize}
\item Static Models (Random Effects/Fixed Effects):\\

\[
y_{it} = \alpha  + X'_{it}\beta + u_{it} \quad i=1,..., N;\enspace t=1,..., T
\]
\\

%Following methods are used:
% \begin{itemize}
% \item Pooling
% \item Random Effects 
% \item Fixed Effects
% \end{itemize}

\item Dynamic Models-GMM for short panel data (Arellano-Bover Estimators):\\

\[
y_{it} = \delta y_{i,t-1}  + X'_{it}\beta + u_{it} \quad i=1,..., N;\enspace t=1,..., T
\]
\\	


\item $y_{it}$ represents the non-performing ratio for bank $i$ in year $t$, \alpha$ is a scalar, $\beta$ is $K \,\times\, 1$ and $X_{it}$ is the \textit{it}th observation on $K$ explanatory variables. The $K$ explanatory variables consist of bank-specific and macroeconomic variables. $\delta$ is a scalar and  $y_{i,t-1}$  is the lagged dependent variable (NPL\_ratio).

\item $ u_{it}$ follows a one-way component model 

\[
u_{it} = \mu_{i} + \upsilon_{it}
\]
\\

where $\mu_{i}$ denotes the unobservable  individual bank-specific effects and $\upsilon_{it}$ denotes the remainder disturbance.

\item $\mu_{i}\;\sim$IID$(0,\,\sigma^2_{\mu})$ and $\upsilon_{it}\;\sim$IID$(0,\,\sigma^2_{\upsilon})$ and the error disturbances $\upsilon_{it}$ are assumed to be mutually uncorrelated $\mathbb{E}(\upsilon_{it}\, \upsilon_{is})= 0\,\, \forall s \neq t$ and not correlated with the individual effects $\mathbb{E}(\mu_{i}\, \upsilon_{it})= 0$.

\end{itemize}

%\subsection{Quantil Regression}



\frame{\frametitle{Pooled Quantil Regression \cite{Koenker.2002} \cite{Koenker.2005}}

\begin{itemize}

\item Quantile regression consider the relationship between the explanatory variables and the dependent variable using the conditional quantile function $Q_{\tau}(y|x)$.

\item Quantil regression shows more robutness in samples with many outliers than OLS and it is semiparametric, i.e. it does not make assumptions about the parametric distribution of the errors.

\item Quantile regression methods also  allows the  analysis of the impact of a set of explanatory variables on the entire conditional distribution of the dependent variable.

\item The quantile $\tau \in (0,1)$ determines the point of $y$ in which the data is split into proportions under $\tau$ and above $1-\tau$. Quantile regression seeks to minimize  the errors $\epsilon_{i}$ that gives asymmetric penalties $(1-\tau)\lvert \epsilon_{i}\rvert$ for overprediction and $\tau\lvert \epsilon_{i}\rvert$  for underprediction.

In other words, $Q_{y} (\tau|\enspace x)$ represents the conditional $\tau$-quantile of $y$ given ($x$). The quantile $\tau \in (0,1)$ determines the point of $y$ in which the data is split into proportions under $\tau$ and above $1-\tau$, that is $F(y_{\tau}=\tau$ and $y_{\tau}=F^{-1}(\tau)$.  Quantile regression seeks to minimize  the errors $\epsilon_{i}$ that gives asymmetric penalties $(1-\tau)\lvert \epsilon_{i}\rvert$ for overprediction and $\tau\lvert \epsilon_{i}\rvert$  for underprediction.

\item The quantile regression estimator in quantile $\tau$ is the one that minimizes the following objective function:
\[
Q(\beta_{\tau})  \,=\,  \sum_{y_{i} \geq x'_{i}\beta}^n \, \tau \lvert y_{i}-x'_{i}\beta_{\tau}\rvert \, +\,+ \sum_{y_{i} < x'_{i}\beta}^n \, (1-\tau) \lvert y_{i}-x'_{i}\beta_{\tau}\rvert

\]
\\
\item The estimator have been showed to be asymptotically normal and bootstrap standard errors are  often used instead of traditional standard errors.

\end{itemize}

\frame{\frametitle{Fixed Effects Panel Quantil Regression (Penalized FE Method) \cite{Koenker.2004} \cite{Koenker.2017}}

\begin{itemize}

\item The penalized least squares interpretation offers the possibility of perform quantile regression models accounting for the existence of individual effects in  panel data.

\item In the Penalized FE Method, the following penalized estimator $(\hat{\alpha}(\lambda),\hat{\beta}(\lambda,\tau)$ is considered
 
\[
\min_{(\alpha,\beta)} \enspace \dfrac{1}{nT} \sum_{k=1}^q \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{t=1}^T \omega_{k} \rho_{\tau}(y_{it}-\alpha_{i}-X'_{it}\beta(\tau_{k}))+ \lambda \sum_{i=1}^n \lvert\alpha_{i}\rvert
\]
\\

where $\omega_{1},...,\omega_{q}$ are nonnegative weigths and $\lambda \geq 0$ is the penalty level.

 \item The idea in this model is to estimate several $\tau$ simultaneously and restrict the individual effect coefficients  $\alpha_{i}$ to be the same for all quantiles and which  values shrinks them toward zero  as  the penalty level $\lambda$ increases. Therefore, the parameter $\lambda$ allows  a calibration of the impact of the penalty.
% 
\item In general, when $\lambda \to 0$ the model is similar to a (weigthed) dummy-variable quantile regression and when $\lambda \to \infty$ the model is more like a (weigthed) cross-section quantile regression, i.e, the lower the value of $\lambda$, the larger the control for fixed effects in the model.

\item The penaly allows a "control" over the many fixed effects coefficients by not letting them reach unrealistic large numbers.



\end{itemize}
}
